# The Compass

*Where STANDOFF is going, and the one story it's built around.*

This is a design document, not a rulebook. It exists so that every decision about the game — a card, a scene, a screen — can be checked against the same thing. Where this and the code disagree, the code is out of date.

---

## The short version

STANDOFF becomes **one story, told over one night**.

Sal Benedetto is arrested on Monday. His trial is next Monday. The crew — you and your friends, under your own names — has seven nights to raise his lawyer's fee and keep thirty years of his secrets out of the District Attorney's hands. Pull it off together and everyone walks away paid. But each of you is also being offered a deal, somebody at the table may already have taken one, and on Monday only one person leaves the richest.

Every night is a different kind of problem: a vote where half the table is lying about why they want what they want; a heist that rewards greed right up until it doesn't; a door you can only talk your way through with clues other people are holding; a small room with a polite prosecutor in it. **Dice** decide what nobody can control. **Cards** break the rules, one sentence each. And **the story remembers**: what you did on Tuesday is waiting for you on Thursday.

The target is a table of people who have never played, shouting at each other inside five minutes and still bringing it up a week later.

---

## What's wrong now

Worth being blunt, because the fixes follow from it.

- **It's a spreadsheet.** Every job ends in a table — *the move / if they hold / if they don't* — and the right answer is the biggest number, discounted by how much you trust the other person. Once you've seen that, you've seen the game.
- **Every round is the same decision.** Hold, fold, or a hedge in between. Different paint on the same wall.
- **Money comes from nowhere.** Twelve thousand dollars for holding the line. Paid by whom? For what? A number with no source has no weight.
- **Nothing is remembered.** Tuesday's betrayal doesn't change Wednesday. The scenarios are good writing that goes nowhere.
- **Too many systems, explained too late.** Heat, markers, pledges, roles, twists, crews, acts, cards. Each defensible; together, a rulebook nobody asked for.
- **Nothing is uncertain.** With the numbers on screen and no dice, the only unknown is the other people. That's the right unknown, but when it's the only one there's nothing to laugh at when a plan goes wrong.

What's right, and worth carrying into the rebuild: the tone, the table look, and people's real names in the story.

---

## The compass: nine rules

When a design question comes up, these settle it, in this order.

1. **Together against the job, alone at the split.** Every night needs the group to pull something off, and gives each person a reason to take more than their share. Both, every night.
2. **Every night is a different kind of decision.** Never the same engine twice in a row.
3. **Talk is the game.** If a scene can be played in silence, it's the wrong scene. Give people information others need, stakes others can't see, and votes others can swing.
4. **The story remembers.** Every choice changes something later. If nothing changes, it wasn't a choice; it was a menu.
5. **Money has a source.** Loot comes out of a job. The Bag is a gym bag on Nonna's kitchen table. Your cut is whatever you didn't put in it.
6. **Not everything is money.** Heat, cards, favours, grudges, secrets, and whether Nonna likes you. Choices that pay in different currencies can't be settled by comparing numbers.
7. **Dice decide what nobody can.** Risk is a roll everybody watches. Cards bend the dice. Nobody's plan survives a two.
8. **One sentence or it's out.** A card, a job, a secret, a scene's instruction: one sentence each. If it needs two, it's two things.
9. **The first five minutes sell the night.** Night One has to land for somebody who arrived late holding a drink.

## The story — *Seven Nights: Sal's Ledger*

### The premise

Read aloud, or shown, at the start. Thirty seconds.

> Monday, 6:10 a.m. Salvatore "Sal" Benedetto — seventy-one, neighbourhood institution, three-time winner of the St. Anthony's tomato fair — is arrested in his bathrobe, in his own garden, holding a watering can.
>
> The charge is tax fraud. The problem is the ledger: thirty-one years of every favour, payoff and debt in this neighbourhood, in Sal's handwriting, and it is no longer where Sal left it.
>
> Assistant District Attorney Wendell Prout wants the ledger. The Castellanos across the river want the neighbourhood. Sal's lawyer wants five hundred thousand dollars by next Monday.
>
> Sal wants you.

### What decides Monday

Two things sit in the middle of the table all night, where everyone can see them.

- **The Bag.** A gym bag on Nonna's kitchen table: money for Morty Klein, attorney at law. It needs to reach its target by Monday. *(The target scales with the table — roughly $100k a head.)*
- **The Case File.** Prout's folder. Every mistake goes in it: an arrest, a witness who's still talking, a page of the ledger in the wrong hands, somebody saying too much in a small room.

On Monday morning the Bag and the Case File set the odds of a single roll of two dice — **the Verdict** — and everybody watches it land.

- **Sal walks.** Everyone on the crew is paid, and whatever's left in the Bag, Nonna splits.
- **Sal goes down.** He names names. Everyone loses half of what they're holding — except anybody who already took Prout's deal.

Then the richest person at the table wins. In one sentence: **most money on Monday wins, and the crew's Monday decides how much everybody has.**

### The cast

Recurring people give the week a spine. Every scene involves at least one of them, and they remember.

- **Sal Benedetto.** In county lockup. Calls once a night. Asks about the tomatoes before he asks about the money.
- **Nonna Benedetto.** Sal's mother, ninety-four. Runs everything from the kitchen. Everyone is afraid of her, correctly. Her favour is worth more than cash, and she knows it.
- **ADA Wendell Prout.** Thirty-four. Runs marathons. Has never lost a case he was given time to prepare. Polite, patient, and offering every one of you a deal, separately.
- **Morty Klein.** Sal's lawyer. The best, priced accordingly. Raises his fee when he gets nervous, and he is a nervous man.
- **Gary Feld.** Sal's accountant, and the only other living person who has read the ledger. Currently in room 14 of the Route 9 Motor Inn, eating from the vending machine and deciding whom to trust.
- **The Castellanos.** The family across the river. Old manners, new appetite. If Sal goes down, the neighbourhood is theirs by Christmas.
- **Dolores.** Runs the diner on Ferry Street. Neutral ground. Knows everything; sells some of it.
- **Detective Ray Mancuso.** On Sal's payroll. Possibly on Prout's. Possibly on the Castellanos'. Ray has a lot of payrolls.
- **Father Dominic.** Hears everyone's confession. Keeps most of it.

### The places

Nonna's kitchen, where the Bag lives · Dolores's diner, neutral ground · three banks on Night One · the evidence warehouse on Pier 9 · the Route 9 Motor Inn · St. Anthony's · the casino boat *Lady Luck* · county lockup · courtroom 4B.

### The week

```
PROLOGUE   ACT ONE: THE MONEY    ACT TWO: THE WITNESS     ACT THREE: THE TRIAL     MONDAY
(arrest)   nights 1-2            nights 3-5               nights 6-7               the Verdict
           ...then the Room      ...then a twist          ...then the Room again
```

**Act One — The Money.** *Get the Bag moving; learn the table.*
Night 1 is always **The Three Banks**, because a public vote with private reasons is the fastest way to make strangers argue. Night 2 draws from **The Night Guard**, **The Bookie's Box** or **The Castellano Wedding** — unless you robbed the Castellanos' own bank on Night 1, in which case it's **The Retaliation**. The act ends in **the Room**: Prout interviews everyone, one at a time.

**Act Two — The Witness.** *Gary is out there. So are the Castellanos. Pages of the ledger start turning up.*
Three nights from a pool — **The Motel**, **The Confessional**, **Nonna's Ring**, **The Armored Car**, **Just One More**, **The Phone Call**, **The River**, **Somebody Talked** — weighted by what has already happened. Midway through, a twist from a small deck: Morty raises his fee; Gary disappears; a ledger page is mailed to the *Harbor Courier*; the Castellanos propose a truce.

**Act Three — The Trial.** *Everything converges.*
Night 6 is **The Last Job**, and which job depends on where you stand: short on money, it's a big score; a fat Case File, it's **The Cleanup** — making evidence disappear. Night 7 is **The Night Before**: a last sit-down at Dolores's, and Prout's final offer, when taking the deal is most tempting and hurts everybody else the most.

**Monday — The Trial.** Nonna makes the crew name the rat. Then the Verdict. Then what happened to everybody. Then the ledger: the scores.

A short game compresses the week to four nights (one, two, one). The full week is seven, around fifty minutes.

### How the story remembers

The week keeps a notebook. Every scene reads it and writes to it.

- **Flags** — facts about the world. *The crew robbed Castellano Credit Union. Lenny the guard saw Andre's face. Gary is hiding in Mo's basement.*
- **Threads** — between players. *Kit owes Mo 20%. Mo holds a grudge against Kit. Andre and Mo swore an oath.*
- **Tracks** — the Bag, the Case File, everyone's heat.

Flags decide which scenes turn up, what they say, and what they offer. A few examples of Tuesday waiting for you on Thursday:

- **You robbed the Castellanos' bank.** Act Two adds *The Retaliation*. Dolores won't serve you. In the Families game, the Castellano players start Act Two with a bonus.
- **The Talker told Lenny the guard they were from the gas company.** On Saturday the gas company is at Nonna's door, and Lenny is on Prout's witness list — Case File +1 — unless somebody pays him a visit.
- **Somebody named Mo to Prout.** Mo starts Act Two with heat, and Prout's next line to Mo is *"Your friend says you drove."* Mo doesn't know which friend.
- **Nonna got her ring back.** On Monday she wears it to court and blesses one die: a reroll on the Verdict.
- **Gary is in Mo's basement.** He eats. Every night he's there, the Bag pays for groceries, and on Night 6 he asks for a car.

At the very end every player gets an epilogue, written from the notebook:

> *Mo put his cut into a bakery on Fifth. It does well. Nobody from the old crew goes there.*
>
> *Andre testified on the Wednesday. He lives in Tucson now, and sends Nonna a card every Christmas. She sends them back.*
>
> *Kit still has the Loaded Die. Nobody will play dice with Kit.*

People will remember the epilogue longer than the score.

## One evening, traced

This is the level of connection we're after: not a plot that branches, but people's choices feeding each other.

**Night 1 — The Three Banks.** Mo's private angle says his cousin works at Harbor Savings, and if the crew picks Harbor he can quietly take forty thousand extra and make the job harder for everyone. Mo argues for Harbor ("the building's falling apart, I've been in there"). The vote ties. Mo holds the Loaded Die. The tie-break comes up 5: Harbor. He works his angle, the job comes off by a single pip, and the take is forty thousand short of what was written on the napkin.

**Counting it out.** Kit plays **Snitch**: *"Did Mo take more than his cut tonight?"* The game answers out loud: **YES.** Kit now holds a grudge against Mo.

**Night 2 — The Night Guard.** Mo is the Talker. Everyone else holds a clue about Lenny the guard. Kit's says *Lenny is a Giants fan, loudly.* Kit looks Mo in the eye and says Jets. Mo opens with the Jets game. Lenny calls it in: two heat for Mo, and Lenny goes on Prout's witness list.

**The fallout.** The game shows every clue exactly as it was written. Kit's card: *Giants.* The table comes apart. Mo now holds a grudge against Kit.

**The Room.** Prout, to Mo: *"A friend of yours tells me you know something about a bank on Harbor Street."* Nobody told him anything. The notebook knows about Harbor, and Prout is fishing. Mo, certain it was Kit, names Kit. Kit takes two heat and is told that somebody named him, but not who.

**Where that leaves the week.** Mo and Kit are a feud with five nights to go. Andre, the only honest vote on Night 1, is the one nobody is watching. None of it was scripted. Each scene handed somebody a reason and a way, and kept the receipts.

That is the whole design in a sentence: **private reasons, public decisions, a way to lie, and a story that keeps the receipts.**

## Who you are

Players stay themselves — real names in the story, the thing that makes this land. On top of that, each person gets exactly three things, always visible on their phone:

1. **A job** — what you do for the crew, with one ability.
2. **A secret** — what you personally want this week, and what it pays.
3. **Two cards.**

Some nights add a fourth, just for that night: **an angle**, a private reason to want something, dealt by the scene.

### Jobs

| Job | Ability |
|---|---|
| **The Driver** | You decide when the car leaves. |
| **The Talker** | When you're doing the talking, you get one extra clue. |
| **The Numbers Guy** | You see exactly who put what in the Bag. Everyone knows you can. |
| **The Muscle** | Once a night, add 2 to a roll. |
| **The Cousin** | Nonna likes you. Once a week, she settles a tie your way. |
| **The New Guy** | You hold four cards instead of three. Nobody trusts you, which is fair. |
| **The Fixer** | Once a week, make a spoken promise binding. The game enforces it. |
| **The Lookout** | In any heist, you see the first alarm die before anybody decides. |

### Secrets

One each, dealt at the start and paid on Monday by whoever in the story cares. Some are greedy, some protect somebody, some are revenge, some are about the story, and one might be the rat. The director only deals secrets the week can pay off — *Nonna's Favourite* is only dealt in a week that includes *Nonna's Ring*.

**Greed**
- **The Skimmer.** Put less than $50k in the Bag all week and still see Sal walk. *+$60k — Sal never checks.*
- **The Collector.** Hold three ledger pages on Monday. *+$80k from a buyer in Jersey.*
- **The Gambler.** Win three rolls you played a card on. *+$40k.*

**Loyalty** — the person you're protecting doesn't know
- **The Soft Spot.** If *{player}* finishes in the top half, you're paid. *+$40k.*
- **The Big Brother.** Keep *{player}* out of jail all week. *+$50k.*
- **Stand-Up Guy.** Never name anyone to Prout. *+$50k from Sal, who hears everything.*
- **Cousins.** Dealt in pairs. You and *{player}* share a grandmother; if you both finish in the top half, she pays you both. *+$40k each.*

**Revenge**
- **The Grudge.** *{Player}* cost you in 2019. If they finish last, you're paid. *+$60k.*
- **The Snake.** Get *{player}* arrested at least once. *+$40k.*

**The story**
- **Nonna's Favourite.** Get Nonna her ring back. *+$50k, and her blessing.*
- **Clean Hands.** Take no heat all week. *+$50k.*
- **The Stirrer.** End the week with three grudges on the table, none of them yours. *+$40k.*
- **Gary's Friend.** Make sure Gary never testifies, any way you like. *+$50k.*

**The traitor**
- **The Rat.** Prout owns you. If Sal goes down and nobody names you on Monday, Prout pays you $150k and your money is safe. You also hold **The Wire**: once a night, quietly add 1 to the Case File.

### Heat, and getting caught

Heat is personal, zero to three, shown as pips on your seat. At three you're **arrested**: half your cash is seized, the Case File goes up by one, and you spend the next night in county lockup with Sal.

**Being arrested does not take you out of the game.** In lockup you get one phone call (tell one player one thing only you know), you can cut a deal with Prout from your cell, and Sal — who is bored — tells you something he shouldn't. You're back the night after with your heat cleared.

## A night, step by step

About five minutes each. The same seven beats every night, so nobody learns a new shape; what changes is the decision in the middle.

1. **The scene.** A paragraph of story on the shared screen, and one line in capitals saying what kind of night it is: *TONIGHT: A VOTE. — TONIGHT: ONE OF YOU TALKS. — TONIGHT: HOW GREEDY ARE YOU?*
2. **The angles.** Private information lands on phones: clues, stakes, temptations. Only you see yours.
3. **The talk.** Out loud, on a generous timer. This is the game.
4. **The move.** The night's one decision. Secret, unless the scene says otherwise.
5. **The roll.** Dice, wherever there's risk. More cards are played here than anywhere else.
6. **The fallout.** What actually happened, written from what actually happened. Money moves, with a source. Heat, the Case File and the notebook update.
7. **Counting it out.** Your cut arrives, and you choose how much of it goes in the Bag. Everyone sees the Bag's total. Nobody sees who put in what — except the Numbers Guy.

Between nights, Sal calls from lockup with a small random twist, everybody draws a card, and sometimes the scene opens a sit-down.

### The one thing that happens every night

Counting it out is the heartbeat, and it's the same every night on purpose, because it's the dilemma the whole week is built on. Money in your pocket is yours, but it can be seized, stolen or blackmailed. Money in the Bag is safe, but it isn't yours any more. And Morty wants installments — the Bag has a milestone at the end of each act, and missing it costs Case File — so "I'll put mine in on Sunday" is a promise. Promises are what this game is about.

## The scene engines

Each night is dressed as a story, but underneath it runs one of these. There are eleven; the pilot needs three.

### 1 · THE PICK — a vote with private reasons
*"Which bank?" Everybody has an opinion. Some of them have been paid for.*

The crew votes between options. Each option has its own story, reward, risk and consequence. Some players are dealt **angles** — a private reason to push one option, honest or not. Ties go to a die, with each option on its own faces.

**The Three Banks** (Night 1, always):

| | First Federal on Main | Harbor Savings & Loan | Castellano Credit Union |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The story** | A vault from 1998 and a manager who's been counting down to retirement since 2011. | The building is older than the alarm, and the alarm is older than the guard. | Across the river, and it belongs to the Castellanos. |
| **The take** | $60k | $120k | $220k |
| **The risk** | 5+ on two dice (83%) | 7+ (58%) | 8+ (42%) |
| **If it goes wrong** | 1 heat each | 1 heat each, 2 for the Talker | 2 heat each |
| **Either way** | — | — | The Castellanos find out. |
| **Tie-break faces** | 1–2 | 5–6 | 3–4 |

Angles dealt tonight, for example:

- *Mo:* **Harbor.** Your cousin Deb is the assistant manager. If the crew picks Harbor you can work an angle during the job: +$40k for you, the target goes up by 2, and the crew's take drops by $40k. Nobody sees it.
- *Kit:* **Castellano.** You owe Vinnie Castellano $40k. If his bank gets hit, the paperwork burns, and so does your debt.
- *Reza:* **First Federal.** Your mother banks there. If the crew hits it, she'll know, and so will Nonna.
- *Andre:* Nothing. You're clean. That makes yours the only honest vote at the table — or the most expensive one.

That last line matters. It tells the one neutral player they're the kingmaker, and invites everybody else to make them an offer.

*Why it makes people talk:* everyone can see the options, nobody can see the reasons, and the person who most wants Harbor has the best argument for it.

### 2 · THE WHISPERS — one person acts, everybody else knows something
*"What do I say to him?" Only the others can tell you, and they might not.*

One player — the Talker — makes a choice. The information to make it well is split across everyone else's phones, one fragment each, and they have to tell the Talker what they know: out loud, in their own words, as truthfully as they like. After the fallout, the game shows every clue exactly as it was written.

**The Night Guard:** Sal's box is on Pier 9, in an evidence warehouse guarded overnight by a man named Lenny. The Talker's phone shows four openings:

- *"Carol sent me — you forgot your pills."*
- *"Gas company. We've had a report of a leak."*
- *"You catch the Jets game? Brutal."*
- *Slide him an envelope.*

The clues, one per player:

- *Lenny's wife Carol left him in March. He doesn't say her name.*
- *Lenny is a Giants fan. Loudly. He has the tattoo.*
- *Lenny took an envelope once, in 2016, and nearly lost his pension. Never again.*
- *Lenny's brother died in the Kearny gas explosion. He'd let a gas man into a burning building.*

The right opening gets you in, and the box has $80k and a ledger page in it. The wrong one and Lenny calls it in: two heat for the Talker, and Lenny goes on Prout's witness list.

The lying is the point, and the week hands people reasons to do it. A player holding a **grudge** against the Talker has every reason to get the Jets wrong. A player whose secret is *The Snake* wants the Talker arrested. The Rat wants Lenny on the witness list. And the reveal afterwards — *"Kit's card said Giants"* — is the moment the table erupts.

Randomness lives here too: in any week, one clue in six is simply wrong. An honest mistake is always a plausible defence, which is exactly what makes a dishonest one survivable.

### 3 · THE GRAB — push your luck
*"One more. It's fine. One more."*

The crew is inside and the money's in reach. Each round, everyone secretly chooses **GRAB** or **GO**. The round's take is split among whoever grabbed. Then an alarm die is rolled, and the alarm gets more sensitive every round. When it trips, everyone still inside is caught: tonight's take gone, two heat each.

**Just One More:** Big Tommy Fusco runs a book on the fight from a back room on the *Lady Luck*. For eleven minutes, during the main event, the room is empty and the box is open.

The Driver decides when the car leaves, and if the car leaves with people still inside, they have to roll to get home.

*Why it makes people talk:* everybody shouts advice they aren't taking.

### 4 · THE PLAN — hidden contributions
*"It needs seven. Everyone put something in. Somebody put in a minus three."*

The job needs a total to succeed. Each player secretly commits something — a card, or effort from nought to three — and some things subtract. The commitments are revealed shuffled, so everyone knows the total and that somebody sabotaged it, but not who.

**The Armored Car:** Thursday, 6:40 a.m., the Brinks truck stops for coffee on Ferry Street, same as every Thursday for eleven years. It takes seven things going right to get the back open.

*Why it makes people talk:* the accusations start before the dice stop.

### 5 · THE ROOM — the prisoner's dilemma, where it belongs
*"Assistant DA Prout would like a word. Separately."*

Each player sits alone with Prout — their own phone, nobody watching — and chooses:

- **Say nothing.** "I want my lawyer."
- **Give him something small.** A harmless detail. Probably harmless.
- **Name somebody.** Pick a player. They take two heat, and they'll know somebody named them, but not who.
- **Take the deal.** Your money is safe whatever happens on Monday, Prout pays you, and the Case File jumps by two. Nobody finds out until the Trial.

If everybody says nothing, Prout has less than he thought and the Case File goes *down*. If one person takes the deal, they're protected and everyone else is worse off. If several take it, Prout only ever needed one.

The Room happens twice a week — at the end of Act One and on the last night — not every round. That is what gives it weight: the old game's entire engine, now the scene everybody dreads.

### 6 · THE SPLIT — divide the bag
*"Everybody says a number."*

**The River:** three hundred thousand dollars in a gym bag on the hood of a car at the end of Pier 12. Nonna's rule, which is older than anybody at this table: everyone secretly writes down how much they're taking. If the numbers add up to more than the bag, the bag goes in the river.

The talking beforehand is the game. The reveal is a scream.

### 7 · THE LIFEBOAT — who do you save?
*No money in this one at all.*

**The Phone Call:** Ray Mancuso rings Nonna's kitchen at 2 a.m. and hangs up after one sentence: they're picking somebody up tonight, and he can't say who. There's time to warn one person. Each player secretly picks one other person to warn. Whoever is warned by the fewest people gets picked up; ties go to a die.

Then everyone sees who warned whom. That's the scene. It feeds straight into the bond web at the end of the night.

**Three Seats** is the heist version: the getaway car has three seats and there are five of you.

### 8 · THE SIT-DOWN — deals
*"Dinner at Dolores's. Everything's negotiable."*

A longer, open talk with tools on the table: **IOUs** (a share of your Monday money, enforced by the game), **card trades**, **Blood Oaths**, **blackmail** if you're holding dirt, and whatever Dolores is selling tonight — twenty thousand to learn one player's secret, say. Anything agreed through the screen is enforced. Anything said out loud is only as good as the person who said it.

**The Castellano Wedding** is the dangerous version: the other family is at the next table, and they're buying too.

### 9 · THE HUNT — somebody talked
*"Nonna wants a name."*

When the Case File jumps and nobody will say why, the crew votes on who talked. Name someone who really did — named names, took the deal, or is the rat — and the Case File drops and they pay for it. Name the wrong person and they collect from Nonna for the insult, and hold a grudge against everyone who pointed.

The Trial opens with a Hunt for the rat, and it's the biggest of the week.

### 10 · THE FAVOUR — not everything is money
*Somebody in the story wants something, and what you do about it matters more than what it costs.*

**Nonna's Ring:** in 1987 Nonna pawned her engagement ring to bail Sal out the first time. It has sat in the window of Castellano Pawn & Loan for thirty-nine years, and on Monday she would like to wear it to court. The crew votes: buy it back out of the Bag, steal it (a roll, and heat), have a fake made (cheaper, and a roll to see whether she notices), or tell her the truth.

What you get isn't money. It's Nonna's blessing — a reroll on the Verdict — or her curse.

### 11 · THE RACE — two families, one target *(Families game)*
*Both crews want the same thing tonight.*

**The Motel:** Gary Feld has been in room 14 of the Route 9 Motor Inn for three days, and both families know it. Each family secretly splits its people between driving, distracting and finding; the dice race; whoever reaches room 14 first decides what happens to Gary. Cousins on opposite sides can tip each other off, at their own family's expense.

## Dice

Randomness belongs everywhere, in the open.

- **Checks are two six-sided dice.** Seven is ordinary. The target is always on screen with the odds in words: *"needs 8 or more — about 4 in 10."*
- **Rolls are public.** The dice are big, on the felt, and everyone watches them land.
- **Die faces carry story.** Tie-breaks put each option on its own faces. Some scenes put something special on a single face — a 5, say — which is exactly what makes the Loaded Die worth holding.
- **Some randomness hides.** Whether a clue is wrong. How sensitive the alarm is. Which twist Sal calls about. What Prout already knows.
- **Cards bend dice.** Loaded Die, Lucky Horseshoe, Black Cat, Let It Ride. A roll isn't settled until everybody has decided not to play anything.

### The Verdict

Monday comes down to one roll: two dice, needing **4 + the Case File** or more. Every $50k the Bag is over Morty's target lowers that by one. Falling short raises it by three — Morty phones it in.

| Case File | The Bag | Sal walks on | Chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $50k over | 4+ | 92% |
| 2 | on target | 6+ | 72% |
| 4 | on target | 8+ | 42% |
| 4 | short | 11+ | 8% |

Name the rat correctly and the Case File drops by three before the roll. Nonna's ring gives one reroll. And somebody at the table is always holding a Black Cat.

## Cards

### The rules for cards

- **One sentence, one verb.** If you have to ask what it does, it's the wrong card.
- **It makes a moment.** Every card should make somebody at the table say something out loud.
- **Face up or face down.** Face-up cards are announced when played. Face-down cards are revealed at the end of the night — or when somebody catches you.
- **Hold three.** Start with two and draw one each night; some scenes hand out particular cards. Too many? Sell one to the Fence.

### The deck

Around thirty-five to start, in five kinds.

**Dice**

| Card | Played | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Loaded Die | face down | The next die anyone rolls comes up 5. |
| Lucky Horseshoe | face up | Add 2 to any roll, after you've seen it. |
| Black Cat | face up | Take 2 off somebody else's roll, after you've seen it. |
| Let It Ride | face up | Reroll any dice. The new roll stands, however bad. |
| Side Bet | face up | Before a roll, call high or low. If you're right, everyone pays you $5k. |

**Votes**

| Card | Played | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Stuffed Ballot | face down | Your vote counts twice. |
| Flip-Flop | face up | Change your vote after the votes are shown. |
| Filibuster | face up | Throw out a vote and hold it again, with no talking. |
| Marker | face up | Call in a favour: one player has to vote the way you do. |

**Information**

| Card | Played | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wiretap | face down | See another player's cards and their secret. |
| Snitch | face up | Ask the game a yes-or-no question about something a player did tonight. It answers out loud. |
| Peephole | face down | See one player's choice before you make yours. |
| Forgery | face down | Swap a clue somebody's about to receive for one you write. |

**Money and heat**

## The social machinery

What makes betrayal cost something without making it forbidden.

- **IOUs.** A share of your Monday money, promised to someone and enforced by the game. Made at sit-downs, extracted by blackmail, sometimes offered to buy a vote.
- **Blood Oaths.** Two players swear. The first to betray the other — naming them in the Room, a Fall Guy, a Black Cat on their roll — pays them half. The game keeps track.
- **Blackmail.** Dirt, or a ledger page with somebody's name on it, lets you demand an IOU or read their secret to the table. It works. It also hands them a grudge.
- **Grudges.** What being wronged gives you. The moment you find out somebody did you wrong — skimmed your cut, fed you a bad clue, named you, blackmailed you, stuck you with their heat, voted you out — you hold a grudge against them: a red thread between your seats. Spend it to **hand them one of your heat**, or to **make your vote against them count twice**. People who've been hurt get a way to hit back, which is what makes the second half of the night louder than the first.
- **Rumours.** Words stamped on seats. Pure social pressure, and cheap to deploy.
- **Paired secrets.** Cousins, soft spots and grudges from 2019: the pacts that exist before anyone has said a word.
- **The deal.** Anyone can become a rat by taking Prout's deal in the Room. The worse Monday looks, the better the deal looks.

The **bond web** at the end already draws who did what to whom. It will have a great deal more to draw.

## Sizes of table

The social shape changes with the number of people, on purpose.

**Two or three — The Partners.** You're what's left of Sal's crew. No rat. The Room is where the tension lives, and the Bag's target is small enough that one greedy night sinks it.

**Four to six — The Crew.** One crew against the week. From five players, one secret is the Rat. Every engine works; the Pick and the Whispers are at their best.

**Seven to ten — The Families.** Two crews: the Benedettos, who need Sal to walk, and the Castellanos, who need him convicted so the neighbourhood falls to them. The Verdict becomes the showdown between two halves of the table.

- Most nights each family plays its own scene. Some nights the families share one (**The Motel**, **The Wedding**), and one night the whole neighbourhood does: in **The Raid** the Feds come for everybody, and the two families can hide each other's things — or not.
- Cousins are dealt across the family line, so two people on opposite sides have a reason to protect each other at their own family's expense.
- Prout offers deals to both families. He doesn't care who wins.
- The individual ranking covers the whole table. A Castellano can finish richest even if the Castellanos lose Monday.

## Making it easy

The biggest complaint is that the game is confusing. This design only works if somebody new can follow it without a single rule explained up front.

- **A new player needs to know three things:** the Bag and the Case File in the middle, the three things on their phone, and the line in capitals that says what tonight is.
- **Everything else is taught by the scene that uses it, in one sentence, the first time.** Night One is the tutorial and nobody notices: a vote, a die, a card, counting it out. The Room teaches itself by being a room. The separate First Night tutorial retires.
- **One decision per night.** Never two buttons that matter at once.
- **Numbers only where they help.** Odds in words. Money with a source. No payoff tables, ever again.
- **The shared screen and the phones do different jobs.** Where there's a TV or a laptop, it shows the table: the story, the dice, the Bag, the Case File, the seats. Phones show only what's private: job, secret, cards, angles, clues and choices. One-device play still works, passing the phone for the private beats.
- **Timers are generous and visible.** Talking is the game, and it shouldn't feel rushed. The host can always skip ahead.

## Starting from scratch

The game is built fresh: new engine, new content, new screens. Nothing from the current game's rules survives by default. The old writing is a quarry, not a foundation — a few of its best lines and people (Father Dominic, the tone, the specificity) will turn up inside the week, rewritten to fit.

What carries over is a direction, not code: the green-baize table look, real names in the story, and the end-of-night ledger with its awards and bond web.

**The one exception worth making is plumbing that isn't the game.** The socket server, the peer-to-peer transport and its broker, the QR encoder, and the seat-and-reconnect logic don't know anything about banks or grudges. They're tested, and each has already had real bugs found and fixed in it: rejoining after a reload never worked, ICE candidates that arrived early were silently dropped, and nobody's name was ever saved. Rewriting them costs a week and re-finds those bugs.

The recommendation: start a clean codebase for the game, lift those modules in as libraries behind a small interface, and rewrite anything that touches rules. If we'd rather rewrite the plumbing as well, the tests that pin those bugs come across first, so the fixes aren't lost.

## How to build it: the pilot first

Don't write seven nights and then find out whether it's fun. Build a **pilot episode**, about twenty-five minutes, and put it in front of real people.

**The pilot — *Monday Night***

1. The prologue.
2. **The Three Banks** — the Pick, with angles, a tie-break die and the Loaded Die.
3. Counting it out.
4. **Just One More** — the Grab.
5. Counting it out.
6. **The Room.**
7. **The Verdict**, and the epilogues.

On a clean codebase, that needs: the scene-engine framework and three engines, dice on the table, the Bag and the Case File, eight jobs, ten secrets, around twelve cards, heat and arrest, and a notebook writing enough flags for the Room, the Verdict and the epilogues to read.

**What to watch when it's played:**

- Did anyone argue inside five minutes?
- Did anybody lie about their angle, and did anyone catch it?
- Did a die make somebody yell?
- Did anyone bring up Night 1 during the Room?
- Could someone who arrived late follow it?

Then, in order: the Whispers, the Lifeboat and the Split; sit-downs with IOUs, grudges and blackmail; the Rat and the Hunt; all seven nights with their flags and callbacks; epilogues; and last, the Families game.

## Open questions

Decisions this document doesn't make, each with a recommendation.

1. **Is Sal's Ledger the spine?** *Recommend yes.* It's the tone the game already has, and it gives every mechanic a reason. Other weeks — Atlantic City, a wedding in Palermo, a cruise — can come later, as seasons.
2. **Is everyone's money visible?** *Recommend hidden until Monday, with rough bands on each seat* — "flush", "getting by", "broke". Hidden cash turns "I need this more than you do" into a claim instead of a fact.
3. **How long is a game?** *Recommend seven nights, about fifty minutes, as the default, and four nights, about thirty, as the short game.*
4. **Is the Rat always in?** *Recommend on by default from five players, and switchable off.*
5. **How hard can one card hit?** Dirt at 20% feels right. Kiss of Death at half of what's seized might be too much. The pilot will say.
6. **Real names only?** *Recommend yes* — it's the thing people love. A nickname field can come later.

## The parking lot

Worth keeping, not for the first version.

- **Seasons.** The next game night picks up from how the last Monday ended: Sal's out and paranoid, or Sal's inside and the Castellanos own the diner.
- **The jury.** In big games, players arrested on the last night sit on the jury instead of sitting out.
- **Sal's voice.** A line of recorded audio for each phone call from lockup.
- **Custom crews.** Name the crew and give everybody a nickname; the writing uses them.
- **A streaming layout.** A shared screen designed to be watched as well as played.
